# PB&J Machine Co. — Product Vision

**Created:** 2026-03-01
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-24

# 📋 Summary

PB&J Machine Co. builds the software interface for a magic sandwich machine — an infinite food source that can produce any peanut butter and jelly sandwich on demand. We didn't invent the magic. We built the layer that makes it useful. Our product makes the machine simple enough that anyone — a parent, a cafeteria worker, a volunteer at a food bank — can put a warm sandwich in a kid's hands without training, without friction, without waste.

# 🌍 The Problem

There are kids who go hungry every day. Not because food doesn't exist — because the systems that deliver it are slow, expensive, and fragile. School lunch programs run out of funding. Food banks depend on donations that come in waves. Parents working two jobs don't have time to cook.

And now there's a machine that can produce an infinite supply of peanut butter and jelly sandwiches from nothing. The raw capability is there. But a magic box sitting on a counter doesn't feed anyone by itself. Someone has to tell it what to make, configure it for a kid with a nut allergy, and order 200 sandwiches ready by 11:30. The magic solves the supply problem. It doesn't solve the access problem.

That's the gap we close.

# 💛 Why This Is Worth Solving

PB&J isn't health food. It isn't gourmet. It's the thing every kid will actually eat — a full stomach and a moment of comfort. There's something deeply right about a world where that sandwich is always available to any child who needs one.

We believe the technology to feed kids shouldn't sit behind complexity. If the machine exists, the only acceptable outcome is that it reaches every kid who's hungry. Not eventually. Now. The barrier isn't the magic — it's the interface. And interfaces are what we build.

# 🖥️ The Product

Our product is the software that sits between the machine and the people who use it. It turns a magical capability into something practical.

For a parent, it's an app on their phone. Pick bread, pick spread, pick jelly, confirm. A sandwich appears in the machine in their kitchen. The whole interaction takes less time than opening the fridge.

For a school cafeteria, it's a batch system. Set the menu for the week — the machine produces on schedule, packaged and labeled, ready for the lunch line.

For a food bank, it's an on-demand station. A volunteer taps a screen, a sandwich comes out. No inventory to manage, no expiration dates to track, no deliveries to coordinate.

In every case, the person using the product doesn't need to understand the machine. They order a sandwich. We handle everything between the tap and the tray.

# 🔭 Where We're Going

The end state is a PB&J machine in every place a kid might be hungry — homes, schools, community centers, shelters, hospitals, after-school programs. The sandwich that's always there.

For the kids it serves, that means no more going hungry because the system failed. For the adults operating it, that means no training, no complexity, no calls to support. A cafeteria worker sets up a lunch schedule once and the machine handles it. A food bank volunteer taps a screen and hands a kid a sandwich. The product disappears into the background and a kid eats.

We measure success in meals delivered, not machines sold.

# 🧭 How We Get There

**Go where the hunger is.** We don't wait for customers to find us. The go-to-market is integration into the systems that already serve kids — school districts, food banks, community organizations. If a school already uses a lunch management platform, we plug into it.

**Build for the operator, not the technologist.** Our users aren't engineers. Every feature we ship gets tested against one question: could someone who's never seen this before figure it out in 30 seconds? If the answer is no, it's not ready.

**Make the first sandwich effortless.** Distribution only works if activation is self-serve. A parent should go from unboxing to holding a sandwich in under five minutes. Every friction point we remove is a kid who gets fed sooner.
